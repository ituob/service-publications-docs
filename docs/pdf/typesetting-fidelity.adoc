== F.32 English: section-by-section diagnosis

The 41-page English PDF from run `44122451030` was compared with the pinned
47-page official fixture above. Both files were extracted with
`pdftotext -layout`; form-feed boundaries were retained so every text fragment
can be attributed to its physical PDF page (including cover and blank pages).

[cols="2,1,1,3",options="header"]

|1. Destination indicators |3–40 |4–41 |The allocation-only template omits printable context rows, including network-relative office suffixes and shared indicators.
|Appendix 1: special origin indicators |41–43 |Absent |Missing section, not denser layout.
|Appendix 2: government telegram organization indicators |44 |Absent |Missing section.
|Appendix 3: cessation notices and amendments |45–46 |Absent |Missing section and amendment form.
|Blank final page |47 |Absent |Blank page is not missing substantive content.

Both main tables span 38 physical pages. The six-page total difference is
therefore not a six-page compression of the office table: the generated file
omits six appendix pages and the official blank final page, while adding a
blank second page. Generated fonts include Arial/Times New Roman, Noto Sans
and STIX Two Math; the official file uses Univers/Arial. A representative shared Afghanistan/Albania page extracted with
`pdftohtml -xml -zoom 1` measures generated body text at 10 pt (Noto Sans),
versus official 8 pt (Arial); median office-code row pitch is 18 pt versus
9 pt, and the left country-column anchor is 60 pt versus 34 pt. These
measurements establish a typography/spacing difference as well as the missing
content, even though the incomplete tables happen to span the same page count.

The comparator parses 1,092 generated table rows versus 1,499 official rows
(1,054 matched). These are extraction counts, not a certified count of missing
records: layout can change parsed rows. Independently, the provider returns
exactly 1,092 allocations plus 1,140 source contexts, including 368
network-relative office and 182 shared-indicator records. Printed examples
`--BA Buenos Aires` and `--BX All others` occur in the official PDF and source
contexts but are absent from the generated table. The comparator's old
legacy-data dispositions are stale for this provider-generated document and
must not be taken as settled classifications of every delta.

The cause is a *real template/projection content gap*. The document invokes
`edition.items`, which contains office allocations; the provider separately
retains printable non-allocation records in `edition.source_contexts`.
The original document also has no appendix template. The verified source
publication already retains those contexts and the official appendix pages.
This differs from the much older 33-page pilot in link:comparison-pilot.adoc[]:
that pilot used the legacy dataset ending at Israel, while this PDF includes
the later offices through Zimbabwe. Increasing font size or adding page breaks
would conceal these omissions rather than repair them.

The repair keeps the canonical 1,092 allocations unchanged and exposes a
separate, original-edition-only `printed_items` projection. It restores 311
printed network-relative office rows and 92 shared office rows (1,495 coded
rows total), aliases, unassigned rows, reserved/telex network notes and country
headings otherwise absent from the allocation list. Source coordinates place
these records in printed order; legacy duplicate contexts and continuation
fragments are not printed twice. The publication's parsed substantive data is
unchanged when the added/corrected source coordinates are excluded.

A shared appendix template restores 28 special-origin network entries, eight
organization indicators with their printed multilingual names, 27 cessation
records and all 30 blank amendment rows in the English, French and Spanish
sources. Focused document specs assert those counts and representative official
values, all canonical office rows, printed order, and missing suffix/alias
regressions. The earlier legacy-dataset truncation is not recreated.

The F.32 shard alone applies link:../../scripts/patch_itu_f32_tables.rb[a guarded
stylesheet patch]: 8 pt Arial table text, 9 pt leading and minimum row height,
zero extra cell top padding, and 12 mm horizontal margins. Crucially, the
service-publication `setTableRowAttributes` template overrides the generic row
style with a 5 mm minimum; both now use 3.175 mm. The earlier generic-only
adjustment left ordinary rows about 15 pt apart, versus 9–10 pt in the official.
Numeric table-note references replace the renderer's alphabetic labels in both
links and note bodies; note targets and prose remain intact. Other publication
shards keep their existing stylesheet.

== What the checks establish

* `pdffonts`: every listed font must be embedded; an empty font list fails.
* `pdftotext -enc UTF-8`: no replacement character, common tofu squares or NUL;
  a known localized register title must round-trip. The reference is the
  publication's explicit `:title:` attribute or AsciiDoc title, falling back
  to the manifest title when source is unavailable. Case, Unicode compatibility
  forms, punctuation and wrapping whitespace are normalized; CJK wrapping
  spaces are removed. An English cover in a Russian or Arabic source also
  requires a known native-script heading to round-trip.
* Cyrillic, Han and Arabic text must retain their script. Arabic presentation
  forms are normalized and bidi controls removed, while logical character
  order is preserved. A reversed Arabic title fails; this is an extraction
  check, not certification of visual joining or mixed-direction cell layout.
* `pdfinfo -box` on every page: portrait A4 within 3 points and zero rotation.
* `pdftotext -bbox`: extracted word boxes must remain within the page, with
  1 point of tolerance. JSON identifies each offending page, word and box.
  This detects text outside the page, including table text; wholly clipped
  text and non-text rules/images cannot be certified through extraction.
* Page counts use link:typesetting-baselines.json[explicit official baselines].
  The F.32 2011 English comparison documents 47 official pages in
  link:comparison-pilot.adoc[the existing comparison pilot]. No official
  page-count baseline exists here for the other 86 PDFs; those are `n/a`,
  never silently counted as official equivalence. `--baseline-dir` can supply
  further official PDFs named `T-SP-<standard>-<year>-<letter>.pdf`.

Real PDF fixtures prove that a nonembedded Helvetica font and extracted tofu
fail. Additional fixtures cover positive controls in all six languages,
reversed Arabic, page rotation/orientation, overflow, page-count mismatch,
missing/unlisted PDFs and artifact layouts. Fixture glyphs are schematic;
they test measurement behavior, not visual typography.

These checks do not certify the cover, ISSN, document numbering, headers,
footers, table rules, language-specific shaping or correspondence of every
cell to an official edition. Those require visual and official-PDF review.
Candidate status remains separate from ITU staff adoption; see
link:refresh-cadence.adoc[refresh cadence].

== Failure causes and targeted changes

* All seven Chinese PDFs initially lose the source title. E.118, E.212A,
  E.218, M.1400 and Q.708A have no Han text at all; E.164D and E.212B retain
  some Han elsewhere but lose the title. The initial CI font step installs
  `NotoSansCJK.ttc`, yet `pdffonts` has no CJK font and titles extract as `#`.
  Fontist can also select `NotoSansSC[wght].ttf` despite a static preference;
  the renderer excludes variable-font paths containing `wght`. The fix
  downloads pinned `Noto Sans SC` Regular and Bold OTFs from `Sans2.004`,
  rebuilds Fontist's index, verifies the registered paths, and requests the
  family in all seven Chinese sources.
  Installation alone is insufficient: metanorma-itu 2.9.4 hardcodes Arial
  on service-publication page sequences and Calibri on table containers,
  overriding the inherited `:fonts:` fallback.
  `scripts/patch_itu_cjk_font_fallback.rb` adds SC to these constant stacks
  in the installed CI stylesheet. It preserves primary fonts, computed
  expressions, weight/style and unrelated stacks, guards the expected
  page-sequence overrides, and logs before/after values. An incompatible
  future stylesheet fails the guard for review. The full hosted result above
  confirms embedded SC fonts and successful title round-trips in all seven.
* Q.708B English, French and Spanish have exactly three extracted underscore
  runs beyond the page on page 178. Each reaches x=601.016 pt; the page is
  595.276 pt wide. These are notification-form blanks, not register rows.
  The template generates long, unbreakable runs of underscores (82–105
  characters); the change caps the four long blanks at 72 characters in each
  language. The bounding-box report retains the actual words and coordinates.
* F.32 English has 41 generated pages versus the historical official 47.
  link:comparison-pilot.adoc[The pilot] dispositions missing source data as
  a major reason for its earlier 33-page generated edition. The current
  41-page difference is a measured mismatch, not proof of a font defect;
  isolating current data and pagination deltas requires the official comparison
  workstream. No arbitrary pagination adjustment is made to force equality.

Arabic titles containing Latin abbreviations require care: Poppler omits a
space at the Latin/Arabic bidi-run boundary (`SANCقائمة`, `MNCفيما`). The
reporter restores that script boundary for comparison, preserving the exact
letter sequence. Mirrored parentheses and typographic punctuation are also
normalized. These are extraction conventions, not missing Arabic glyphs.
